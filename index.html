01 / CAMERA AS AN INFORMATION MACHINE

Our question

Why haven’t cameras improved as much as other information technologies?

Once computation becomes part of the camera, the recovered image no longer has to be the physical image formed by a lens. Measurements across time, aperture, spectrum and illumination can be combined to estimate the scene.

We co-design optical architecture, electronic focal planes and algorithms to push photography toward the physical limits of resolution, speed and spectral diversity.

Launching November 2026

One imaging system.
Designed end to end.

Hong Kong Camera Lab bridges physical optical engineering, mathematical sampling theory and photonic computing to create next-generation imaging architectures.

Instead of separating optical design from downstream computation, we co-design wavefront shaping, active optomechanical control, adaptive non-uniform sampling and optical neural processing. The goal is to improve resolution, dynamic range, field of view, throughput and energy efficiency as properties of the complete system.

Optical field→Adaptive capture→Physical processing→Computational inference

Research directions

Capture more.
Infer better.

01

COTS array camera testbeds

Rapid multi-aperture prototypes with synchronized capture, parallax-robust data fusion, synthetic aperture reconstruction and neural multi-view synthesis.

02

Adaptive camera modules

Compact fast-optics systems integrating active focus, dynamic aberration correction and advanced micro-optics packaging.

03

Optical neural processing

Diffractive and hybrid optoelectronic platforms that perform feature extraction and data reduction within the optical path.

04

Adaptive computational imaging

Sampling theory, task-driven acquisition and hardware-in-the-loop validation for efficient information capture.

05

Wide-field high-resolution systems

Gigapixel microscopy and multi-aperture platforms for biomedical imaging, industrial inspection, robotics and scientific sensing.

Programmatic roadmap

From testbed to field validation.

A three-phase research program connects mathematical foundations, custom hardware and complete imaging systems.

Phase 01Q4 2026 – Q2 2027

Foundation

Deploy modular COTS array testbeds, implement baseline data-fusion algorithms and establish adaptive sampling models.

Phase 02Q3 2027 – Q1 2028

Custom fabrication

Integrate compact adaptive camera modules, construct optical neural processing testbeds and assemble multi-aperture microscopy systems.

Phase 03Q2 2028 – Q4 2028

System convergence

Validate high-throughput optical front ends and unified adaptive imaging platforms with research, biomedical and industrial collaborators.
